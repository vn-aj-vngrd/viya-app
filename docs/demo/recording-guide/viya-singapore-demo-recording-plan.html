Why the demo matters

The problem is not planning. It is proving the trip is ready.

Travelers already juggle emails, PDFs, receipts, notes, calendars, and screenshots. Viya turns that scattered proof into connected workspace state.

Research signal: Expedia Group describes the traveler path to purchase as often complex and reports 303 minutes plus 141 pages of travel content before booking.
1Scattered proof

Flights, hotels, receipts, passes, reminders, and notes live apart.

2Disconnected plans

Itinerary tools usually do not know what documents or budget items support the plan.

3Readiness gap

The traveler still asks what is confirmed, what is missing, and what to do next.

Audience takeaway: The demo is judged by whether Viya turns loose travel context into visible readiness, not by whether it can write a generic day-by-day list.

Final audience notes

What to remember after the recording.

  • AI is focused. Viya acts as a trip-only agent, not a generic chat screen.
  • Changes are reviewable. The user stays in control while AI stages workspace updates.
  • Proof matters. Vault uploads ground bookings, receipts, and readiness checks.
  • Readiness is the product. The final state is a prepared trip, not just a pretty itinerary.
Closing line: Viya helps travelers stop asking, "Did I miss anything?" and start traveling with a workspace that proves what is ready.
