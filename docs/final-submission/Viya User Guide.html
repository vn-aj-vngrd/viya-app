1. Get started

Begin from the intro, then open sign-in.

Viya starts with a short carousel that explains the full trip lifecycle: dream it, get ready, and remember it. Tap Get started to open authentication.

  1. Review the intro. The carousel shows planning, preparation, and recap.
  2. Tap Get started. The sign-in sheet opens without leaving the app context.
  3. Choose Google. For this demo, use Continue with Google.
  4. Return to Home. After login, Viya loads the personalized dashboard.

2. Sign in

Use Google and choose the saved account.

Tap Continue with Google. iOS may ask permission for Expo to use google.com for sign-in. Continue, then choose vanajvanguardia@gmail.com from the Google account picker.

This guide uses the already signed-in Google account on the simulator: vanajvanguardia@gmail.com.

3. Confirm access

Finish Google login and land on Home.

Accept the iOS sign-in permission if shown, then tap Continue on Google's confirmation screen. Viya returns to Home after authentication completes.

4. Home

Start with context, not a blank form.

Home shows active trips, suggested prompts, and destination ideas. Use it to continue an existing trip or start a new one from inspiration.

Best first action: open the active trip if one exists. Otherwise, start a trip from the create flow or ask Viya.

5. Create a trip

Give Viya the basics, then let the workspace take over.

The trip setup flow collects the destination, travel window, budget comfort, traveler context, pace, and preferences. Keep answers practical; Viya can refine details later.

Helpful inputs Destination or region Dates or rough timing Budget ceiling Trip purpose Pace and must-haves
Demo example: "Plan my Singapore group business-conference reward trip for Tech Week. Keep it polished but not exhausting, and handle flights, hotel, event pass, budget, itinerary, readiness, and one victory-lap moment."

6. Ask Viya

Use AI as an operator for the trip.

Ask Viya from the global chat or inside a trip. Inside a trip, Viya uses the current workspace as context, so prompts can be short and specific.

Viya should not silently overwrite trip data. Review suggested changes before applying them.

Useful prompts

  • Build a realistic itinerary from this trip profile.
  • Review this budget against my planning ceiling.
  • Organize Vault and identify missing proof.
  • Audit readiness before departure.
  • What is the smartest next thing to check?

Good AI behavior

  • Asks only for missing context.
  • Explains the evidence used.
  • Creates reviewable drafts.
  • Connects changes across modules.

7. Workspace

Move between modules without losing the trip story.

The overview is the command center. It surfaces readiness, remaining budget, module counts, and the next best action. The switcher lets users jump to itinerary, budget, Vault, and readiness quickly.

Use Overview when deciding what to do next. Use the switcher when you already know which module needs work.

8. Itinerary and budget

Turn the plan into days and numbers.

Itinerary gives the traveler a day-by-day route with stops and pace. Budget keeps the trip grounded in confirmed spend, daily room, and receipts.

Review before travel Arrival and departure buffers Conference or event anchors Hotel and transport proof Daily safe spend Receipt links

9. Vault and readiness

Finish when the trip is actually prepared.

Vault stores confirmations, receipts, passes, and notes. Readiness turns those files and decisions into concrete tasks so the user knows what is done and what is missing.

Readiness is the user-friendly finish line: when blockers are resolved, the trip is ready to travel.

What to add to Vault

  • Flight, hotel, transfer, and activity confirmations.
  • Event passes, tickets, IDs, visas, and insurance notes.
  • Receipts and expenses for budget tracking.
  • Readiness notes such as packing, arrival cards, or emergency details.

What readiness checks

  • Documents and booking proof.
  • Budget buffer and receipt evidence.
  • Schedule risks and transfer gaps.
  • Assigned preparation tasks.
